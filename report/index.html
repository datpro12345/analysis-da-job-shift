A study of public job postings

How the Data Analyst job changed, 2022 to 2026

Read this first: limits and risks

  • Small samples. United States: 2,545 postings in 2022 and 6,381 in 2026. Vietnam: 141 in 2022 and 242 in 2026. Only big shifts show in Vietnam.
  • Not national statistics. The postings are a sample from job platforms and public datasets, not the whole job market.
  • The platform mix may have changed. On the Vietnamese platform, the Data Analyst share of all postings rose 6.6 times from 2025 to 2026. The mix of postings may have changed, so part of a rise may not be a change in the job.
  • Mentions, not requirements. The study counts postings that name a skill. It does not show that employers require it.
  • Different periods. Vietnam 2022 covers the whole year. The US 2022 sample is November to December only.
  • Weak differences. A difference with a q-value above 0.10 is not confident. Do not quote it as a change.
  • Exploratory. The study is not peer reviewed.
  • Non-commercial. Results are CC BY-NC 4.0. Use them for non-commercial purposes only.

Five shifts in one minute

Each number is the share of postings that mention the item, in the earlier period and in the later one. "pp" means percentage points.

How to read this

Five things to know before you quote a number
  • Mentions are not requirements. A posting counts if it names the item at least once. "Python is a plus" and "Python is required" count the same. So each percentage is an upper bound on how many employers require the skill. Say "mention", never "require".
  • Cell sizes. United States 2022: 2,545 postings. United States 2026: 6,381. Vietnam 2022: 141. Vietnam 2026: 242. Only postings whose job title is in the Data Analyst family are counted.
  • The bars around each dot are 95 % confidence ranges. A long bar means little data. If the bars of the two periods do not overlap, the change is probably real. "Confident" below means it survives a correction for testing 92 skills at once (false discovery rate q of 0.10 or less).
  • The Vietnam 2022 sample is small. With 141 postings (45 from one platform), a skill at 20 % in 2022 needs to move by about 13 points before the test can see it. "No clear change" there means "too little data to tell", not "no change".
  • The sources are not identical. The two US periods come from different datasets, and Vietnam 2022 mixes a short-text platform with longer archived pages. The "mix-matched" column adjusts for seniority (and region in Vietnam), and a same-method check for the US agrees on direction. Sizes of change are less certain than directions.

AI and LLM terms

"Any AI mention" counts the word AI, "artificial intelligence", ChatGPT, Copilot, LLM and similar. A mention does not say what the job holder does with AI. Using AI tools, building AI products and a company blurb about "AI-first" all count. Splitting these is the job of stage 2 (see Next).

What the text says that counts cannot

A topic model lets the postings group themselves, with no skill list given. Each theme below is a group of words that tend to appear together. The names are a plain-English reading of the top words. The bars show the share of postings whose strongest theme is this one. A theme that comes back every time the model is refitted has stability near 1.

Leftover boilerplate shows up as themes. Equal-opportunity notices and one employer's footer text survive cleaning and form their own themes. They are listed under each model so you can see what the cleaning missed. They say nothing about the role.

Method in plain words

  1. Clean the postings. Remove personal data, repeated footer lines and duplicates the same way in every source, so two eras compare fairly.
  2. Read them with rules. A list of 91 skills and tools, with English and Vietnamese patterns, marks each posting yes or no per skill. Every hit traces to one pattern in a versioned file.
  3. Let a model read them (next). A language model will label tasks, the role AI plays, and whether a skill is required, each with a word-for-word quote as proof.
  4. Test the differences. Each skill is a share of postings, compared between two periods. The page shows 95 % Wilson intervals, an exact test, a false-discovery correction across 92 skills, effect sizes, a bootstrap range, a re-weighting for seniority mix and a repeat at matched sample sizes.
  5. Let topics emerge. TF-IDF and NMF group postings into themes, refitted three times to check that a theme comes back.

Full design with worked examples: technical design document. Requirements: business requirements.

Limits

  • Mentions, not requirements (see above).
  • None of the sources is a random sample of the market. They are public datasets of data-role postings, one Vietnamese job platform and archived portal pages.
  • Vietnam 2022 has 141 postings. Only large shifts are visible there.
  • Longer adverts name more tools. Vietnamese platform texts are shorter than US ones, so part of any rise can be longer text. A per-1,000-characters check is planned.
  • Rules miss misspellings and new product names, and a few generic words may still match.
  • The study shows what changed. It cannot show why. Do not read these numbers as "AI caused this".
  • The Vietnamese platform's year field cannot be checked against the original pages.
  • The Vietnamese platform's 2026 mix looks different. Data Analyst postings are 0.078 % of all its postings in 2025 and 0.518 % in 2026, 6.6 times more. The same-platform comparison may therefore not compare the same mix of postings.

Next: stage 2 and 3

Stage 2, in progress as a design: a language model labels every posting with tasks, the role AI plays (a tool the analyst uses, a product the team builds, governance, or only a company blurb), and whether each skill is required, preferred or only mentioned. Two annotators label a fifth of the postings and agreement must reach kappa 0.60 first.

Stage 3: a report with mentioned and required side by side, and rates per 1,000 characters. This page will be deepened with those results.
